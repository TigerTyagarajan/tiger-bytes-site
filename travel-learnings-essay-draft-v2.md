# What the World Has Taught Me

In April 2025, we started our life in Lisbon. It is our fourth country and our ninth city as residents — Mumbai, Chennai, Delhi, Gurgaon, Newtown in Connecticut, Scarsdale, New York City, London and now Lisbon — and it is our twentieth home in more than thirty-five years. People sometimes ask about the disruption of moving so often. I think about the opposite. The learning of new cultures, food, history, language and sports is so much to look forward to in every move. And then there are the new friends, who you nurture and preserve for life. That is the joy of these constant moves.

Looking back on a corporate life of thirty-eight years, its joys were mostly about constantly learning new things, asking questions and exploring answers. But the other joy was experiencing new cultures — the food, the way people live, their beliefs, their attitudes — all in service of enjoying and admiring diversity, because I am convinced that is the human superpower over millennia. A life of travel, done properly, is not a collection of places. It is an education.

### Coimbatore — December 2023

Travel trains you to notice where the energy is moving. A couple of days in Coimbatore, in December 2023, the fifteenth largest city in India, showed me the economic vibrancy of tier-3 and tier-4 Indian cities: crowded malls, hospitals and dental clinics, a metro rail under construction, digital payments everywhere, every place packed with activity. The next ten years could see the Indian economy become the engine of global growth — and you can see that future early if you go and look beyond the obvious addresses.

### Delhi — December 2023

History is the first teacher, and it is not behind us; it keeps explaining the present. I ended 2023 at the Red Fort in Delhi, watching the sound-and-light show enact the close to four hundred years of the fort's history, while listening to Empire, the podcast by Anita Anand and William Dalrymple, and relating many of the subplots of that history to the world around me. So much to learn from history as we live through a complex geopolitical and social landscape everywhere in the world.

[Photos: Delhi — delhi-red-fort-night-jan-2024.jpg · delhi-red-fort-performance-jan-2024.jpg]

### Boston — March 2024

Boston, in March 2024, taught the same lesson as Coimbatore at the other end of the world. It is a city that exudes innovation, be it in AI and machine learning, medicine and health, or technology and capital. The energy comes from walking the hallways of Harvard, Boston University and MIT, meeting young minds dreaming and disrupting. It is a city that shows we can all hope for a better world with everything these young minds are working on.

[Photos: Boston — boston-mit-dome-mar-2024.jpg · boston-mit-sloan-atrium-mar-2024.jpg]

### Wales — May 2024

Every place on earth has a unique and deep history, that you can learn from. A first trip to Wales, in May 2024, taught me how much of it can sit inside a few green days: the meadows, rivers and lakes, and then the prehistoric tomb of Pentre Ifan going back more than five thousand years; chapels and castles a thousand years old, each with its own history; and, in the same small country, the origins of the Industrial Revolution, with coal mining and the first use of steam engines.

[Photos: Wales — wales-pentre-ifan-may-2024.jpg · wales-castle-may-2024.jpg]

### Istanbul — September 2024

I felt it again in Istanbul, in September 2024. It is a favourite city, standing at the crossroads of civilization for thousands of years — uplifting in its mixture of cultures, food, colours and spices.

In Belgrade, in October 2024, I found myself in the middle of the deep, seven-thousand-year history of Serbia and the Balkans — fascinated, intrigued and curious, as always. The Nikola Tesla Museum there stayed with me; it would come back to me months later, in another city.

That same month, October 2024, I had my first immersion in Lisbon, and called it a cultural melting pot with tons of history and connections to Brazil, India, Africa, Japan and more. I did not yet know it would become home.

### Lisbon becomes home — April 2025

In April 2025 Lisbon did become home. A week into living here, I had fallen in love with the city and the country. The people are so nice — everyone, so polite and so welcoming — something the world can learn from. The ten-mile run around Monsanto park felt like Central Park or Hyde Park on steroids.

### Warsaw — May 2025

In Warsaw, in May 2025, on my ten-mile exploratory run through a city I was visiting for the first time, I came to the Copernicus Science Museum, and it reminded me of the Nikola Tesla Museum in Belgrade: great scientists and geniuses on whose shoulders today's tech breakthroughs are built. Everything is an evolution — life, ideas, science, culture.

### Japan — November 2025

Places also teach you how people choose to live. I have thought deeply about why I love Japan so much, and the answer is a list of virtues practised daily: a deep and complex history; gardens and greenery in spite of a dense population; the people, so respectful in their attitude and behaviour; long-term thinking in everything; the importance given to trust and relationships; minimalism — neat, simple, not loud and garish; perfection, exactness and cleanliness in everything. In a crowded street in Tokyo or Kanazawa, in November 2025, twenty people will wait two minutes at a pedestrian crossing without crossing, even though there are no vehicles in sight. You can set your watch to Shinkansen departures, and in four minutes a whole train is cleaned completely and turned around for the next trip. It is ikigai and zen thinking.

[Photos: Japan — japan-reflecting-pool-nov-2025.jpg · japan-shinkansen-platform-nov-2025.jpg · japan-zojoji-tokyo-tower-nov-2025.jpg]

### Singapore — November 2025

Singapore, revisited in November 2025 after a gap of more than twenty-five years, taught a different lesson. The smoothest touchless, zero-human-interaction immigration I have seen anywhere — and as an Indian passport holder who has traveled to almost a hundred countries, I should know. A global melting pot in a tiny geographic footprint; high-rises punctuated by green zones and trees, often fifty stories high; and a safety poster at a construction site with slogans in English, Chinese, Tamil and Bengali — where else would you find this? But it is obviously a city where everything can be monitored and discipline is a cultural way of life. I may never want to live there, given the temperature and humidity, but I certainly plan to go back, and not wait another twenty-five years.

[Photos: Singapore — singapore-safety-banner-nov-2025.jpg · singapore-marina-bay-nov-2025.jpg]

### Sri Lanka — January 2026

None of this learning is passive. In 2025, exploration was at the heart of everything I did every day: time in twenty-nine cities in fourteen countries on three continents — the people, the culture, the history, the arts, the architecture, the food and wine, the archaeology — asking tons of questions and learning.

[Photo: 29-cities-2025-collage.jpg — the 2025 travels corkboard, placed here per Tiger's instruction Nov 7 ("where I say that as an example of this travel based learning on 2025")]

The questions are the method. Starting 2026 on a vacation in Sri Lanka, in January 2026, I found myself asking: why is Sri Lanka a tea-drinking and not a coffee-drinking country? Ninety percent tea drinkers, and per-capita tea consumption that dwarfs coffee. Why? History: the coffee blight of the 1860s destroyed all the coffee on the island. Geography: ideal tea-growing conditions. Economics: Sri Lanka is now the fourth largest tea producer in the world. Culture: tea became the drink of home and tradition. One question at a street stall, and a whole country explains itself — its past, its land, its livelihood, its habits.

And what a country to ask questions of. Sri Lanka lies so close to Chennai, where my ancestors hail from, and there are so many similarities — yet it is so different: steeped in Buddhism, its language a near-cousin of Tamil and yet its own. The damage from the recent cyclone is still visible, and it leaves you asking a harder question: what will happen to such island nations as climate change wreaks its havoc? These days, asking questions of an AI in real time as I travel multiplies the learning tenfold, but the questions still have to be mine.

[Photos: Sri Lanka — sri-lanka-sage-statue.jpg · sri-lanka-vatadage.jpg]

### Óbidos — May 2026

An hour from Lisbon sits Óbidos, which I visited in May 2026: a medieval walled town of three thousand residents with fourteen dedicated bookstores, a UNESCO City of Literature, where I jumped from book to book the way we now jump from link to link, down very interesting rabbit holes.

[Photos: Óbidos — obidos-church-bookshop-may-2026.jpg · obidos-castle-walls-may-2026.jpg]

### Australia — August 2026

Australia, where I spent a month in August 2026, teaches with landscape instead of cities: phenomenal diversity of nature, flora and fauna, because an island continent separated from other land masses for millions of years allowed evolution to play out intriguingly differently. You do not just see a place like that. You study it.

[Photos: Australia — sydney-opera-house-night-aug-2026.jpg · sydney-sandstone-aug-2026.jpg]

### Lisbon, sixteen months on — August 2026

Sixteen months into Lisbon, in August 2026, three things have proven true. Time feels experientially so short when you are curious — learning, and passionate about the people you meet every day — and so long when it is boring, tired and same-old, with no passion or learning. My instinct that I would fall in love with the people I meet day to day in Portugal has turned out to be completely true. And new friends in new places, with new food and wine and new history and culture to learn from and about, is what keeps one young and healthy in mind and body.

That, in the end, is what decades of travel have taught me. Move towards what you do not understand. Read the history under your feet. Watch how people live, and let it change how you think. Ask the simple question — why tea, and not coffee? — and follow where it leads. Diversity is the human superpower. Stay curious, and the world keeps teaching.
